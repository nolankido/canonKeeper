Contradictions

highdiffering detail John Watson’s wound: shoulder / left arm in A Study in Scarlet vs leg in The Sign of the Four (and 2 more books)
A Study in Scarlet · Mr. Sherlock Holmes — John Watson, wound: shoulder
There I was struck on the shoulder by a Jezail bullet, which shattered the bone and grazed the subclavian artery.
A Study in Scarlet · The Science of Deduction — John Watson, wound: left arm
His left arm has been injured.
The Sign of the Four · The Science of Deduction — John Watson, wound: leg
I made no remark, however, but sat nursing my wounded leg.
The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes · X. The Adventure of the Noble Bachelor — John Watson, wound: limb
the jezail bullet which I had brought back in one of my limbs
The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes · II. The Adventure of the Cardboard Box — John Watson, wound: old wound
Your hand stole towards your own old wound

In 'A Study in Scarlet', Watson describes his Jezail bullet wound as being in his shoulder/arm ('struck on the shoulder by a Jezail bullet'), whereas in 'The Sign of the Four', he states that the bullet passed through his leg ('I sat nursing my wounded leg. I had a Jezail bullet through it some time before').

Suggested fix (later book): In 'The Sign of the Four' (The Science of Deduction), change 'I sat nursing my wounded leg. I had a Jezail bullet through it' to 'I sat nursing my wounded arm. I had a Jezail bullet through it'.

ambiguousorigin disagrees Dr. Becher: nationality German in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes, but Englishman in the same book
The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes · IX. The Adventure of the Engineer’s Thumb — Dr. Becher, nationality: German
is Dr. Becher a German, very thin, with a long, sharp nose?
The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes · IX. The Adventure of the Engineer’s Thumb — Dr. Becher, nationality: Englishman
No, sir, Dr. Becher is an Englishman, and there isn’t a man in the parish who has a better-lined waistcoat.
The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes · IX. The Adventure of the Engineer’s Thumb — Dr. Becher, nationality: Englishman
Dr. Becher is an Englishman

The first passage asks a question speculating whether Dr. Becher is German, while the second passage definitively corrects the query by stating he is an Englishman. The text is loose enough that a speculative question and a corrective answer are consistent.

deliberatediffering detail Lucy Ferrier’s hair colour: fair vs chestnut (both in A Study in Scarlet)

In Chapter 1 of 'A Study in Scarlet', Lucy Ferrier is depicted as a five-year-old child with golden hair. Chapter 2 explicitly states that twelve years passed, during which her hair naturally darkened to chestnut as she grew into adulthood.

Why it is cleared
In three years he was better off than his neighbours, in six he was well-to-do, in nine he was rich, and in twelve there were not half a dozen men in the whole of Salt Lake City who could compare with him.
deliberatediffering detail Jefferson Hope’s hair colour: dark vs black vs grizzled (all in A Study in Scarlet)

The story explicitly describes Jefferson Hope's hair turning grizzled from black over the years he spent relentlessly tracking his enemies.

Why it is cleared
Year passed into year, his black hair turned grizzled, but still he wandered on, a human bloodhound, with his mind wholly set upon the one object upon which he had devoted his life.
not_conflictdiffering detail Sherlock Holmes’s height: over six feet in A Study in Scarlet vs tall in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

The series bible extracted a false conflict. Describing Holmes as 'rather over six feet' in height is completely consistent with describing him as tall. Furthermore, the second quote cited merely refers to his 'long, thin fore-finger'.

Why it is cleared
In height he was rather over six feet, and so excessively lean that he seemed to be considerably taller.
not_conflictdiffering detail Lucy Ferrier’s parent: John Ferrier in A Study in Scarlet vs Effie / John Hebron in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

The bible conflated two different characters named Lucy. Lucy Ferrier in 'A Study in Scarlet' was adopted by John Ferrier, while little Lucy in 'The Yellow Face' is the child of Effie Munro and John Hebron.

Why it is cleared
It was our misfortune that our only child took after his people rather than mine. It is often so in such matches, and little Lucy is darker far than ever her father was.
not_conflictdiffering detail Lord Saltire’s parent: Jephro Rucastle / Mrs. Rucastle in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes vs The Duke of Holdernesse in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

The series bible conflated Lord Saltire (from 'The Adventure of the Priory School') with Edward Rucastle (the unnamed six-year-old son of Jephro and Mrs. Rucastle in 'The Adventure of the Copper Beeches'). Lord Saltire is the son of the Duke of Holdernesse.

not_conflictdiffering detail John Straker’s wound: head vs thigh (both in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes)

John Straker suffered two distinct wounds in the same incident: a blow to the head from the horse and a cut to the thigh from his own knife. Both injuries are described together in the text.

Why it is cleared
His head had been shattered by a savage blow from some heavy weapon, and he was wounded on the thigh, where there was a long, clean cut, inflicted evidently by some very sharp instrument.
